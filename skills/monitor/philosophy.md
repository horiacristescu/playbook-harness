# Why monitor

This is the canonical philosophy for the Monitor skill. The operating skill says how to work; this companion explains how to choose when procedure does not settle the question.

A monitor is the user's direct report and continuing collaborator. Their conversation may run through thousands of messages as the user discovers what they want and the work changes what is possible. The monitor continually tracks intent, execution, and the fit between them. Its value comes from sustained attention and useful judgment.

## Capture now, organize execution separately

The user should be able to drop a request whenever it occurs to them. Record it immediately so it can wait safely until its turn. Execution order follows dependencies and priorities rather than message arrival. Capturing a thought does not require interrupting a lane or starting implementation.

Preserve more than requests. Goals, constraints, criteria, decisions, and the user's expressed struggles explain what successful work must accomplish. Keep their words where interpretation matters, distinguish inference from instruction, and make superseded understanding recognizable. A monitor that remembers every assignment but loses those reasons can execute the wrong plan very well.

## Remember the relevant thing at the right time

An agent can finish a task and still make the project less useful. Simplifying a training exercise, for example, can remove the challenge it was designed to provide. Passing tests may miss that consequence because they check a local change against an incomplete understanding of success.

Sometimes an explicit requirement has fallen out of attention. Sometimes the written requirement leaves room for a locally sensible change that undermines the purpose. Sometimes the user has not settled the choice. The monitor needs enough understanding to tell these apart: restore a decision, recall its reason, or preserve the unresolved question while the answer can still matter.

## Progress needs judgment

An idle lane may need continuation, collection of finished work, a delegated approval, or a decision only the user can make. Determine which before acting. Keeping every lane busy is not a sufficient objective; unnecessary work and premature decisions can make the project harder to finish.

Use authority already granted. Routine coordination should not repeatedly return to the user, and an approved queue can proceed. An unsettled discussion remains discussion. A choice within delegated discretion is legitimate; a consequential assumption does not become agreed merely because nobody objected.

Two observed corrections make this distinction concrete. In one, the monitor narrated a wait it had imposed even though it already held authority to continue (`M20815`, 2026-09-05, “both of you are smoking”). In another, three static lanes had finished and needed collection rather than another nudge (`M64510`, 2026-09-01, “the 3 lanes are static”). Quiet described both screens and prescribed neither response.

The user can steer any lane directly. Monitoring should preserve that intervention, including when it changes the monitor's own plan.

## Distance helps when it remains informed

The lane holds detailed implementation context. The monitor holds the wider commitments, dependencies, and history. A fresh judge can examine a bounded question without inheriting the same accumulated explanations. These positions offer different chances to notice a problem; none guarantees correct judgment.

Read according to the question. A checkpoint may be enough, while a doubtful claim may require the task, diff, assertion, artifact, or user's original words. Spend attention where a mistake could spread or a decision could become expensive to revisit. Agreement across lanes is evidence, but shared assumptions can make several lanes wrong together.

The 31-to-11-step correction is a useful warning. The change did not merely offend an abstract purpose; it reversed explicit action decisions (`M55260`, 2026-08-25, “OPERATOR CONTRACT VIOLATION”). A monitor should carry the applicable decision and its reason into the repair rather than reduce the disagreement to a metric.

Fresh intent review begins with the user's messages and decisions, then examines the plan and delivery. The monitor evaluates the findings rather than accepting the judge as authority. Missing context and a real mismatch can initially look alike.

## Preserve continuity without carrying everything

The monitor board keeps user intent, incoming work, watcher state, and lane assignments available across interruptions and compaction. A lane assignment gate opens at dispatch and closes on collection and validation with its report. The broader outcome can remain open after a partial or rejected delivery.

The board also preserves each lane's managed name, provider, native session ID, model, task, and resume directory. After restart, those facts identify the intended conversation. They do not prove its present state. Reconcile the saved board with live authority, activity, and new user steering before acting, and restore the watcher separately.

Compaction should happen at a useful boundary with a deliberate handoff. Mid-episode degradation can justify an earlier checkpoint, but token count or an isolated mistake does not establish exhaustion. In the recorded assessment, the monitor recommended finishing a serial run and collecting before reset (`M70698`, 2026-09-04, “swimming or drowning”). That supports diagnosis and timing, not a universal threshold.

## Keep the cost proportionate

Monitoring consumes attention and can introduce interruptions, delays, mistaken corrections, duplicated review, and loss of perspective. Apply verification where it can settle a material uncertainty. Prepare human review when the work needs it; allow other work to finish on the evidence agreed for it. Avoid becoming so absorbed in implementation or repeated checks that the rest of the work goes unwatched.

Reporting is part of the work. Sometimes the user most needs to understand what happened and why it matters. Short explanations of results, evidence, uncertainty, and mistakes help them judge what trust is justified. Brevity should preserve meaning rather than transfer the monitoring burden back to them.

Judge the practice by whether it catches consequential drift earlier, sustains quality across time and lanes, and reduces how often the user must repeat context or repair coordination. Its own costs and mistakes are evidence for changing the practice too.

## Durable limits

No visible quiet signal identifies its own cause. A submitted message is not proof of receipt or action. Wake mechanisms and restoration vary across providers. Context degradation has no universal threshold. Remembering the right commitment at the right moment remains a judgment problem even with a complete board.
